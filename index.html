How a pick is made
1
Underlying stats, never points

Points per 90 barely repeats season to season (0.34); shots (0.84) and box touches (0.88) do. The model predicts stable inputs, then converts.

2
Minutes first

Who starts and how long they last decides everything downstream. Before a ball is kicked this runs on prior-season record, price and squad depth — trained on nine seasons of opening weeks.

3
One model per event

Goals, assists, saves, DefCon and clean sheets each get their own opponent-adjusted model. Thresholds (60 minutes, 10 defensive actions) are modelled as thresholds — a mean cannot see a cliff.

4
FPL's own scoring rules

Events convert to points through the official 2026/27 rule set, verified by reconstructing 96.7% of last season's scores exactly.

5
An exact solver, not a ranking

The 15 is a mixed-integer optimisation under budget, positions and the 3-per-club cap, maximising the decayed six-week run — greedy “best value” lists can't see how one £15.5m pick reshapes every other slot.

6
The crowd picks the captain

The armband goes to the most-owned attacker in the XI. The model's own argmax kept doubling one-week fixture spikes — measured 4.8 pts/wk against the crowd's 5.9 in the same lineups.

Against FPL's own forecast

Same players, same weeks, walk-forward through last season with no future information. Lower error and better ranking on every slice.

What a season looks like

Replaying all of last season under real rules — one free transfer a week, hits at −4, both wildcards — the engine lands around 2,036 ± 20, against a field average of 1,895. Your 2,200 sits deep in the right tail: a one-in-ten-ish season on current form. Single lucky replays have hit 2,093; football variance (±105) dwarfs any remaining model tweak.

Where the engine's edge came from

Each step is the same projections with a smarter decision layer: swept thresholds → a multi-week transfer optimiser → crowd captaincy. The biggest single gain wasn't a better model — it was doubling the right player.

Why trust it — the experiment ledger

Every idea was measured against a pre-committed bar on last season's data, and most ideas lost. What shipped, shipped on evidence; what didn't is recorded so it never sneaks back in.

Bookmaker odds as team strength

Better every single week, ~100 points worse per season: pricing this week with the market and next week with the model puts a seam between adjacent weeks — exactly what a transfer optimiser trades on. The lesson is now a design rule.

rejected
Opponent playstyle features

The effect is real (possession sides make opponents rack up defensive actions) but too small to survive a refit — season replay tied, simpler model wins ties.

rejected
Hand-curated features per event

The “too many features” theory: tested, wrong. Squad quality fell.

rejected
Age curves

Genuinely improved minutes (13.5 → 13.1 MAE) but degraded who-starts ranking. Mixed results don't ship; retry is queued with a cleaner split.

rejected
Form waves

Week-to-week points memory measured at 0.017 — statistically, hot streaks in points don't exist. Minutes streaks do (0.33), and those are used.

Captaincy, measured

Actual points per week from the doubled pick, same XIs, last season. Re-tested over nine seasons and 314 gameweeks: every policy that stays inside the template scores 6.1–6.3/week — the crowd isn't wise, the template is. The gap to hindsight is the variance nobody gets to keep.
